# Current methodology and limitations

Signal Scout is an international research workspace built from selected source feeds, not a complete social-listening or market-intelligence corpus. Scheduled records currently combine individually licensed Stack Exchange questions, licensed Typst and Fedora forum metadata, licensed reporting/analysis feeds, and separately labeled official or collaborative context. Optional browser-direct searches cover Mastodon, Lemmy, GDELT, Wikimedia talk pages, and Stack Exchange. Wikinews is excluded because its editions are read-only archives since 2026-05-04. Each source has its own access, language, retention, and coverage limitations; see the app's Sources page and [the source register](SOURCES.md).

Lead review currently shows exploratory, query-selected views—not a single global word-of-mouth measure. Exact Stack Exchange tags are counted among eligible sampled questions over a short window and compared with observed-day baselines; the Q&A archive can omit matches beyond per-query page caps. A separate phrase-overlap view links Stack Exchange tags or collector-selected search phrases, and exact two-to-four-word excerpts from Fedora topic titles, to same-language licensed reporting/analysis headlines from the preceding seven days. Only overlaps present in at least three distinct discussion records are displayed; isolated matches are withheld. Stack Exchange search-phrase matches must also appear in the question title; Fedora excerpts are generated from titles and are not platform tags. Typst topics are not currently part of this overlap view. Matching uses exact token boundaries; ambiguous short acronym-only phrases and title excerpts containing listed English stopwords are suppressed to reduce generic phrase collisions. This heuristic can also omit relevant phrases. The view does not translate, stem, classify sentiment, determine source independence, infer influence/causation, or assess financial materiality. These are review prompts only; they do not create an evidence-qualified `research_leads` record.

The manual research brief lets a person select source links and record a working thesis, alternatives, counter-evidence, and what would change their mind. These notes are researcher-authored, not model-generated conclusions. Saved briefs are private. Public lead records are shown only when evidence and alternatives are present, the stored independent-source count is at least two, and linked documents resolve to at least two reviewed source operators. Multiple Stack Exchange sites or Global Voices/Conversation editions collapse to their shared operator; unknown discovery feeds do not count toward independence. This operator check does not prove topic relevance, temporal baselines, or a causal thesis; those still require human review.

Counts describe only observed, selected samples; they are not estimates of public opinion, investor attention, source independence, or a market-wide signal. Matching language is required for the phrase-overlap view, so it misses translations and paraphrases. Different platforms, query terms, indexing, publication dates, licensing filters, and caps create selection bias. No trading recommendation, causal claim, or historical performance claim is supported.
