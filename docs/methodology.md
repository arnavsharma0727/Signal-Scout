# Current methodology and limitations

Signal Scout is an international research workspace built from selected source feeds, not a complete social-listening or market-intelligence corpus. Scheduled records currently combine individually licensed Stack Exchange questions, licensed Typst and Fedora forum metadata, licensed reporting/analysis feeds, and separately labeled official or collaborative context. Optional browser-direct searches cover Mastodon, Lemmy, GDELT, Wikimedia talk pages, and Stack Exchange. Wikinews is excluded because its editions are read-only archives since 2026-05-04. Each source has its own access, language, retention, and coverage limitations; see the app's Sources page and [the source register](SOURCES.md).

Lead review currently displays only active, manually reviewed `research_leads` records whose cited documents pass the source-operator and counter-evidence checks. Production has no active records and there is no in-app or scheduled workflow that promotes discovered themes into this queue. The codebase contains experimental exact-tag and phrase-overlap helpers, but they are not a current user-facing lead-generation process. Counts and literal phrase matches do not measure investor attention, source independence, sentiment, influence, causation, or financial materiality.

The browser-local lead dossier lets a person select source links and record a working thesis, alternatives, counter-evidence, and what would change their mind. These notes are researcher-authored, not model-generated conclusions. For its local qualification checklist, every recent citation must be explicitly assessed as supporting, contradicting, context, or not relevant; every relevant source must be attested as opened and checked against its original; and each must include a source-specific paraphrase. The checklist also requires at least three recent relevant citations, at least two reviewed source operators, at least two attributed conversation/Q&A citations with displayed byline labels and no single label over 60% of that sample, a discussion item plus a reporting or expert-analysis item, supporting and contradicting evidence, an alternative explanation, and a disconfirmation test. A displayed byline is not identity verification, and multiple labels do not prove multiple people. Passing creates a local, human-reviewed dossier for sharing; it is not an independently verified finding and does not publish a production lead. Saved briefs are private when account storage is configured; otherwise drafts and dossiers remain in the browser or can be exported locally. Public lead records are shown only when evidence and alternatives are present, the stored independent-source count is at least two, and linked documents resolve to at least two reviewed source operators. Multiple Stack Exchange sites, Bluesky language searches, Lemmy federated indexes, and Mastodon server views each collapse to one provider class; Global Voices/Conversation editions similarly collapse to their publisher. Unknown or unresolved publishers do not count toward operator diversity. These labels do not prove topical independence, temporal baselines, or a causal thesis; human review remains necessary.

Counts describe only observed, selected samples; they are not estimates of public opinion, investor attention, source independence, or a market-wide signal. Matching language is required for the phrase-overlap view, so it misses translations and paraphrases. Different platforms, query terms, indexing, publication dates, licensing filters, and caps create selection bias. No trading recommendation, causal claim, or historical performance claim is supported.
