# Current methodology and limitations

Signal Scout is an international research workspace built from selected source feeds, not a complete social-listening or market-intelligence corpus. Scheduled records currently combine individually licensed Stack Exchange questions, licensed Typst and Fedora forum metadata, licensed reporting/analysis feeds, and separately labeled official or collaborative context. Optional browser-direct searches cover Mastodon, Lemmy, GDELT, Wikimedia talk pages, and Stack Exchange. Wikinews is excluded because its editions are read-only archives since 2026-05-04. Each source has its own access, language, retention, and coverage limitations; see the app's Sources page and [the source register](SOURCES.md).

Explore can request Bluesky's current provider-ranked trend labels without an account or key. These labels only help a researcher choose a phrase for a separate source sweep; they are transient browser metadata, not citations, geographically targeted trends, a representative sample, evidence of attention, or qualified leads. The sweep and subsequent original-source review are explicit user actions.

Production currently has no evidence-qualified active lead. The authenticated researcher publish/withdraw workflow and migration `0015_research_lead_authorship.sql` are deployed and the migration is installed, but sign-in remains disabled because the GitHub OAuth provider and `SUPABASE_AUTH_ENABLED` deployment setting are not configured. The publication path has not yet been exercised against a real reviewed lead. Once enabled, the server reloads every cited row by database ID and enforces the current rights-reviewed source constructors, 72-hour eligibility, at least three distinct citations and two reviewed operators, discussion plus reporting/expert analysis, attributed conversation evidence with no dominant byline label, original-source attestations, source-specific notes, support and counter-evidence, an alternative, and a disconfirmation test. Publication creates an invisible draft, links every checked citation, and activates it only after those links succeed. The authenticated author can withdraw their own lead; withdrawn records remain out of the public queue. These gates are procedural safeguards, not validation of representativeness, factual interpretation, sentiment, influence, causation, or financial materiality.

After the workflow is enabled, a researcher may explicitly publish reviewed links from Bluesky, Mastodon, or Lemmy with only a public permalink, minimal byline/date/language metadata, and the researcher's paraphrase; post text is discarded. Stack Exchange citations are rechecked server-side and accepted only when the current official API record confirms the canonical title, author, date, freshness, and CC BY-SA 4.0 license; the question body is not requested or stored. These rows are publicly readable. GDELT and Wikimedia results cannot be promoted through this path. This avoids silently retaining external post text or treating client-supplied metadata as verified evidence. A passing local checklist is not itself a public lead; publication requires sign-in, review of the exact public disclosure, and explicit confirmation. Existing production coverage may not satisfy the server gate, and an empty queue is valid when it does not.

The lead queue separately reports scheduled licensed forum/Q&A topic and question records from the prior 24 hours, grouped only by reviewed operator. It excludes Wikipedia talk-page revision metadata and does not count visitor-triggered Bluesky, Mastodon, Lemmy, or GDELT results. These are source-pipeline diagnostics, not a complete forum sample, audience measure, or lead.

The browser-local dossier lets a person select source links and record a working thesis, alternatives, counter-evidence, and what would change their mind. These notes are researcher-authored, not model-generated conclusions. Every recent citation must be explicitly assessed as supporting, contradicting, context, or not relevant; each relevant source must be attested as opened and checked against its original and have a source-specific paraphrase of at least 20 characters. The checklist also requires at least three recent relevant citations, at least two reviewed source operators, at least two attributed conversation/Q&A citations with displayed byline labels and no single label over 60% of that sample, a discussion item plus reporting or expert analysis, both supporting and contradicting evidence, an alternative explanation, and a disconfirmation test. Topic and thesis must be substantive; alternatives and disconfirmation text must each contain at least 20 characters. A displayed byline is not identity verification, and multiple labels do not prove multiple people. Passing creates a local dossier; publishing is a separate authenticated and confirmed action. Saved briefs are private when account storage is configured; otherwise drafts and dossiers remain in the browser or can be exported locally. Unknown or unresolved publishers do not count toward operator diversity. These labels do not prove topical independence, temporal baselines, or a causal thesis; human review remains necessary.

Counts describe only observed, selected samples; they are not estimates of public opinion, investor attention, source independence, or a market-wide signal. Matching language is required for the phrase-overlap view, so it misses translations and paraphrases. Different platforms, query terms, indexing, publication dates, licensing filters, and caps create selection bias. No trading recommendation, causal claim, or historical performance claim is supported.
