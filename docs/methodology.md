# Current methodology and limitations

Signal Scout is an international research workspace built from selected source feeds, not a complete social-listening or market-intelligence corpus. Scheduled records currently combine licensed Stack Exchange questions, licensed reporting/analysis feeds, and separately labeled official or collaborative context. Optional browser-direct searches cover Mastodon, Lemmy, GDELT, Wikimedia talk pages, and Stack Exchange. Wikinews is excluded because its editions are read-only archives since 2026-05-04. Each current source has its own access, language, retention, and coverage limitations; see the app's Sources page and [the source register](SOURCES.md).

Lead review currently shows two distinct exploratory views. First, exact Stack Exchange tags are counted among eligible sampled questions over a short window and compared with observed-day baselines; the query-selected Q&A archive can omit matches beyond per-query page caps. Second, a phrase-overlap view links a CC BY-SA 4.0 Stack Exchange community tag or a scheduled connector search phrase with same-language licensed reporting/analysis headlines from the preceding seven days. Community-tag matches use the source-applied tag; scheduled-query matches require the phrase in both the question title and headline and are explicitly labeled collector-selected. Phrases use exact token boundaries, including short acronyms such as `AI` that require extra validation; the view does not translate, stem, classify sentiment, determine source independence, infer influence/causation, or assess financial materiality. Both views are review prompts only; neither creates an evidence-qualified `research_leads` record.

The manual research brief lets a person select source links and record a working thesis, alternatives, counter-evidence, and what would change their mind. These notes are researcher-authored, not model-generated conclusions. Saved briefs are private; public lead records remain filtered unless verified evidence, alternatives, and linked source documents are present.

Counts describe only observed, selected samples; they are not estimates of public opinion, investor attention, source independence, or a market-wide signal. Matching language is required for the phrase-overlap view, so it misses translations and paraphrases. Different platforms, query terms, indexing, publication dates, licensing filters, and caps create selection bias. No trading recommendation, causal claim, or historical performance claim is supported.
