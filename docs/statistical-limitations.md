# Statistical limitations

Signal Scout is descriptive and triage-oriented. User-selected companies, aliases, markets, source sets, topics, and time windows create selection and coverage bias. Different platforms represent different populations. Local terms can be ambiguous; translations can lose nuance; publication and indexing times can differ. Small samples can produce unstable gaps even after smoothing and minimum thresholds. Syndication can create dependent observations. A locally prominent topic may be immaterial, while a material event may be absent from configured sources. Many scans create chance outliers through multiple testing. No causal or out-of-sample return claim is made.
# Daily coverage metric interpretation

`metrics_daily` is a descriptive coverage table, not an alpha, sentiment, or divergence score. A row is scoped to one UTC date, entity, market, and source class. `document_count` counts entity-linked records; `unique_content_hash_count` counts distinct normalized content hashes. It does not merge syndicated copies or semantically similar stories across URLs; a real story-cluster model is future work. `independent_domain_count` counts distinct publisher domains. `effective_domain_sample_size` is the inverse Herfindahl concentration of those domains, so repeated records from one domain do not look like many independent sources. `first_seen_in_window_utc` is the earliest observed publication timestamp in the available trailing 30-day window, not an all-time first-seen claim. The trailing median and MAD use only days with observed records; absent days are not assumed to be zero, and baselines stay null until 14 prior observation-days exist.

`topic_stance_mix_json` stays null until a validated classifier exists. `evidence_status=insufficient` means the row is below the fixed minimum of 20 unique hashes or 5 domains. `descriptive_only` means those minima are met; it does not establish representativeness, statistical significance, causal impact, or Korea/U.S. divergence. News and forum classes are never pooled by this aggregation.

## Exact-tag Stack Exchange observation baseline

The research queue compares each exact original Stack Exchange tag's share among unique eligible questions published in the last 72 hours with the median daily share on observed publication dates in the preceding 30-day archive. Days without any eligible questions are omitted rather than filled with zero. Baselines remain unavailable until 14 prior observed dates exist; the median absolute deviation (MAD) is shown as a robust spread description, not a significance test or alert threshold. Tags are normalized for case and Unicode form and combined only by exact tag string across the selected Stack Exchange communities. This remains a query-selected sample from one expert-Q&A platform, not a measure of public attention; one question can carry multiple tags, and saved-question counts can reflect changing API/query coverage.

The Lead review page may add a **sample-level review flag** under methodology v1.2, but it does not create or promote a `research_leads` row. The fixed exploratory gate requires at least 3 exact-tag questions in the recent 72-hour sample, at least 20 eligible questions overall, at least 2 Stack Exchange communities, at least 14 prior observed publication days, and a recent tag share at least `max(3 × prior MAD, 15 percentage points)` above its prior daily median. This is a deterministic triage prompt, not a significance test, population trend, cross-platform confirmation, or market/causal thesis. Multiple tag checks and query/index changes can still create apparent shifts; inspect every linked question and seek independent news and discussion evidence. A qualifying prompt stays distinct from the evidence-qualified lead queue.

## Literal discussion–reporting phrase overlap

The current cross-source review view looks only at the preceding seven days. A Stack Exchange question must be individually marked CC BY-SA 4.0 and pass the title-query eligibility check. Phrases may come from source-applied community tags or the scheduled connector's own search terms; the latter are additionally confirmed in the question title and clearly marked collector-selected. The phrase must occur at token boundaries in a same-language licensed-analysis/reporting headline. Punctuation is normalized; stemming, synonyms, translations, and semantic similarity are absent. Two-character collector terms such as `AI` are allowed only as whole tokens and are especially broad. One source document is counted once per phrase. At most three question and three headline links are shown per phrase. This detects literal co-mentions in a query-selected sample; it does not establish common event identity, editorial independence, discussion-to-news influence, unusual frequency, causality, sentiment, market relevance, or investment value. Missing overlaps are not evidence of absence.
